
# Mean (Average)

## What is Mean?

The **Mean**, also called the **Average**, is one of the most common measures of central tendency.

It tells us the **average value** of a dataset.

To find the mean, we:

1. Add all the values together.
2. Divide the total by the number of values.

The mean gives us a single value that represents the **center** of the data.

---

## Formula

```text
Mean = (Sum of all observations) / (Total number of observations)
```

---

## Example

Suppose five students scored the following marks:

```text
50, 60, 70, 80, 90
```

### Step 1: Add all the marks

```text
50 + 60 + 70 + 80 + 90 = 350
```

### Step 2: Count the total number of students

```text
Number of Students = 5
```

### Step 3: Divide the total by the number of students

```text
Mean = 350 / 5

Mean = 70
```

**Answer**

```text
Mean = 70
```

---

## Outliers and Mean

An **outlier** is a value that is much larger or much smaller than the other values in the dataset.

For example,

```text
50, 60, 70, 80, 90
```

Mean:

```text
(50 + 60 + 70 + 80 + 90) / 5 = 70
```

Now suppose one student's mark is entered incorrectly as **500**.

```text
50, 60, 70, 80, 500
```

New Mean:

```text
(50 + 60 + 70 + 80 + 500) / 5

= 760 / 5

= 152
```

Notice that the average changed from **70** to **152**, even though only one value changed.

This happens because the **mean uses every value in the dataset**.

For this reason, we say:

> **The mean is affected by outliers.**
