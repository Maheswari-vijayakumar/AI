
# Median

## What is Median?

The **Median** is the **middle value** in a dataset after arranging the values in **ascending or descending order**.

It divides the dataset into **two equal halves**:

- 50% of the values are below the median.
- 50% of the values are above the median.

Unlike the mean, the median is **not affected by outliers**. This makes it a better measure of central tendency when the dataset contains extremely high or low values.

---

## How to Find the Median?

### Case 1: Odd Number of Observations

If the dataset has an **odd** number of values, the median is the **middle value**.

### Example

Arrange the data:

```text
10, 20, 30, 40, 50
```

There are **5 values**, so the middle value is:

```text
Median = 30
```

---

### Case 2: Even Number of Observations

If the dataset has an **even** number of values, the median is the **average of the two middle values**.

### Example

Arrange the data:

```text
10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60
```

The two middle values are:

```text
30 and 40
```

Calculate the average:

```text
Median = (30 + 40) / 2

Median = 35
```

---

## Outliers and Median

Let's look at an example.

Now replace **90** with **500**.

```text
50, 60, 70, 80, 500
```

The median is still:

```text
70
```

Notice that even though one value changed from **90** to **500**, the median did **not** change.

This is because the median only depends on the **middle value**, not on how large or small the other values are.

For this reason, we say:

> **The median is not affected by outliers.**

---

## Advantages

- Easy to understand.
- Not affected by outliers.
- Works well for skewed data.
- Represents the middle value of the dataset.

---

## Limitations

- The data must be sorted before finding the median.
- Does not use every value in the dataset.
