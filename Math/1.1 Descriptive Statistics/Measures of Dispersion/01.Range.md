
# Range

## What is Range?

The **Range** is the simplest measure of dispersion.

It tells us **how spread out the data is** by calculating the difference between the **largest** value and the **smallest** value in a dataset.

Unlike the mean, median, and mode, which describe the **center** of the data, the range tells us **how far apart the values are**.

A **small range** means the values are close together.

A **large range** means the values are spread far apart.

---

## Formula

```text
Range = Maximum Value − Minimum Value
```

---

## Example

Suppose the marks of five students are:

```text
50, 60, 70, 80, 90
```

### Step 1: Find the maximum value

```text
Maximum = 90
```

### Step 2: Find the minimum value

```text
Minimum = 50
```

### Step 3: Apply the formula

```text
Range = Maximum − Minimum

Range = 90 − 50

Range = 40
```

**Answer**

```text
Range = 40
```

---

## Outliers and Range

Let's look at another example.

Dataset:

```text
50, 60, 70, 80, 90
```

Range:

```text
90 − 50 = 40
```

Now replace **90** with **500**.

```text
50, 60, 70, 80, 500
```

New Range:

```text
500 − 50 = 450
```

Notice that changing just **one value** increased the range from **40** to **450**.

This happens because the range only depends on the **smallest** and **largest** values.

For this reason, we say:

> **The range is highly affected by outliers.**

---

## Advantages

- Very easy to calculate.
- Easy to understand.
- Gives a quick idea of how spread out the data is.

---

## Limitations

- Uses only two values (minimum and maximum).
- Ignores all the other observations.
- Highly affected by outliers.
- May not accurately represent the overall spread of the data.

---

## Key Points

- Range measures the spread of a dataset.
- It is calculated as **Maximum − Minimum**.
- A small range means the data is close together.
- A large range means the data is widely spread.
- Range is highly affected by outliers.
