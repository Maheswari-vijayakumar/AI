# Variance

## Why Do We Need Variance?

In the previous sections, we learned about **Range** and **Interquartile Range (IQR)**.

Both measures help us understand how spread out the data is, but they have some limitations.

- **Range** only uses the smallest and largest values.
- **IQR** only uses the middle 50% of the data.

Neither of them considers **every observation** in the dataset.

To measure the spread of the **entire dataset**, we use **Variance**.

Variance tells us **how far each value is from the mean (average)**.

The larger the variance, the more spread out the data is.

The smaller the variance, the closer the values are to the mean.

---

## What is Variance?

**Variance** is a measure of dispersion that tells us **how much the values in a dataset differ from the mean**.

Instead of looking only at the smallest, largest, or middle values, variance uses **every value** in the dataset.

Because it considers every observation, variance gives a much better picture of the overall spread of the data.

## Why Do We Square the Differences?

When we subtract each value from the mean, some differences are positive and some are negative.

Example:

```text
Mean = 30

10 − 30 = -20
50 − 30 = +20
```

If we simply add these differences,

```text
-20 + 20 = 0
```

The spread disappears.

To avoid this problem, we **square each difference**.

Squaring also gives more importance to values that are far away from the mean.

---

## Example

Suppose we have the following dataset:

```text
10, 20, 30, 40, 50
```

### Step 1: Find the Mean

```text
Mean = (10 + 20 + 30 + 40 + 50) / 5

Mean = 30
```

---

### Step 2: Find the Difference from the Mean

| Value | Mean | Difference |
|------:|-----:|-----------:|
|10|30|-20|
|20|30|-10|
|30|30|0|
|40|30|10|
|50|30|20|

---

### Step 3: Square Each Difference

| Difference | Squared Difference |
|-----------:|-------------------:|
|-20|400|
|-10|100|
|0|0|
|10|100|
|20|400|

---

### Step 4: Find the Average of the Squared Differences

```text
Variance = (400 + 100 + 0 + 100 + 400) / 5

Variance = 1000 / 5

Variance = 200
```

**Answer**

```text
Variance = 200
```

## Understanding Variance

Imagine two classrooms.

### Classroom A

```text
48, 49, 50, 51, 52
```

All students scored almost the same.

Variance is **small** because the marks are close to the mean.

---

### Classroom B

```text
10, 20, 50, 80, 90
```

The marks are spread out.

Variance is **large** because the values are far from the mean.

---

## Advantages

- Uses every observation in the dataset.
- Gives a complete measure of data spread.
- Widely used in statistics and machine learning.

---

## Limitations

- The unit of variance is **squared**.

Example:

If marks are measured in **points**, variance is measured in **points²**.

This makes variance difficult to interpret.

To solve this problem, we use **Standard Deviation**, which is simply the square root of the variance.

---

## Key Points

- Variance measures how far the data is spread from the mean.
- It uses every observation in the dataset.
- Larger variance means greater spread.
- Smaller variance means the data is closer to the mean.
- Variance is measured in squared units.
- Standard deviation is introduced to make the result easier to understand.
